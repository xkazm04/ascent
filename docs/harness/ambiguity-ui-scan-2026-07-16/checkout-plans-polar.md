# Checkout & Plans (Polar) — ambiguity+ui scan (2026-07-16)
> Total: 5 (Critical: 0, High: 2, Medium: 3, Low: 0)

## 1. Post-checkout return param `?credits=pending|error` is produced but consumed nowhere — the buyer gets zero feedback after paying
- **Severity**: High
- **Category**: missing-state
- **File**: `src/app/api/billing/checkout/route.ts:96` (producer), `src/app/org/[slug]/page.tsx` (non-consumer)
- **Scenario**: Checkout 303-redirects the returning buyer to `/org/<slug>?credits=pending` on success and `?credits=error` when session creation fails. A grep across `src/app/org/**` finds no reader of the `credits` search param — the org page renders identically with or without it. A user who just paid real money lands on the dashboard with no "payment received, credits arriving shortly" banner (and since fulfilment is webhook-async, the balance chip may still show the OLD number); a user whose checkout failed sees nothing at all and doesn't know the click silently died.
- **Root cause**: The redirect contract was designed on the API side (route even centralizes `orgUrl(status)` so success/error "can't drift") but the UI half was never built — the param is only asserted in `route.test.ts:153`, never in a component.
- **Impact**: The single most anxious moment in the product (money left the buyer's account, entitlement not yet visible) has no state. Confused buyers double-purchase or file support tickets; failed-checkout users churn without knowing anything went wrong. For plan purchases the misnamed `credits=` param compounds it (see #5's sibling note): even a future banner keyed on it would say "credits" after a tier upgrade.
- **Fix sketch**: In the org page (or `OrgHeader`/`CreditsControl`), read `sp.credits`: `pending` → dismissible info banner "Payment received — your credits/plan will appear within a minute" (optionally poll the balance endpoint once or twice); `error` → error banner "Couldn't start checkout, you were not charged — try again." Strip the param on dismiss via `router.replace`. Rename to a neutral `billing=` status while touching both sides.

## 2. Refund fraction mixes denominators and treats a zero-gross order as fully refunded — a partial refund (or a $0 discounted order) can wrongly revoke a paid tier
- **Severity**: High
- **Category**: edge-case-gap
- **File**: `src/app/api/billing/webhook/route.ts:192-193,212`
- **Scenario**: `fraction = refundedAmount / (netAmount > 0 ? netAmount : totalAmount)`, and `gross <= 0 → fraction = 1`. Two undocumented assumptions: (a) that Polar's cumulative `order.refundedAmount` is denominated against `netAmount` — if Polar refunds against the charged total (tax/discount-inclusive `totalAmount`) while `netAmount` excludes tax, a large-but-partial refund yields `fraction >= 1` and the `fraction >= 1` branch at line 212 drops the org's plan to `free` even though the customer still holds the subscription (the code's own comment says a partial refund must NOT revoke). (b) A 100%-discounted plan order (gross 0 — e.g. a promo/comp) that later emits any `order.refunded` event hits the `: 1` fallback and revokes the tier for a $0 reversal.
- **Root cause**: The denominator choice and the `gross==0 → treat as full refund` fallback carry no recorded reasoning about which Polar amount field `refundedAmount` is actually cumulative against; the clamp `Math.min(1, …)` hides overshoot rather than flagging it.
- **Impact**: Money-adjacent tier state: a paying Team/Pro customer who receives a partial goodwill refund (or bought under a 100% coupon) can be silently floored to `free` mid-period — the exact "strip a tier they've paid for" bug the canceled-at-period-end handler explicitly avoids.
- **Fix sketch**: Verify against Polar docs/sandbox which field `refundedAmount` accumulates against and use that same field as the denominator (likely `totalAmount`); document it at the computation. Gate the tier revoke on `refundedAmount >= <that same gross>` directly rather than the derived clamped fraction, and for `gross <= 0` log-and-skip the downgrade (nothing of value was paid, so nothing to claw) instead of defaulting to "full refund".

## 3. plans.ts declares "no dollar amounts are invented here" then hardcodes $10/$20 that /pricing sells — with no reconciliation against the real Polar prices
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Category**: trade-off-undocumented
- **File**: `src/lib/plans.ts:4-5,51,63`; rendered at `src/app/pricing/page.tsx:40-45,81`
- **Scenario**: The file header states "Pricing itself lives in the billing provider (Polar…) — this map is feature/allotment metadata, not the price book, so no dollar amounts are invented here", yet `monthlyPrice: 10` (pro) and `monthlyPrice: 20` (team) are exactly invented dollar amounts, surfaced as the headline price on the public pricing cards and in the SEO `metadata.description`. The actual charge is whatever the Polar product mapped via `POLAR_PLAN_PRODUCTS` costs — two sources of truth with no cross-check, and the header comment actively tells a maintainer the second source doesn't exist.
- **Root cause**: The display-price constants were added after the header was written; the trade-off ("we duplicate Polar's price for a static public page and accept drift") was never recorded, and the pricing page's own comment (lines 34-38) claims the numbers "can never drift from what's actually charged" — true only versus plans.ts, not versus Polar.
- **Impact**: A price change made in the Polar dashboard (the operationally natural place) leaves /pricing advertising the old number — a customer clicks "Get started" at $10 and is charged the new amount at checkout: a trust/compliance problem, and the misleading comments make the drift hazard invisible in review.
- **Fix sketch**: Minimum: fix both comments to state the real contract ("monthlyPrice is display-only and MUST be updated in lockstep with the Polar product price"). Better: at startup or in a scheduled check, fetch the mapped Polar products' prices via `getPolar()` and log/alert on mismatch with `PLAN_FEATURES[plan].monthlyPrice`; or render the price from the Polar catalog with plans.ts as fallback.

## 4. Subscription revoke/refund always floors the org to `free`, clobbering a manually-granted higher tier
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Category**: edge-case-gap
- **File**: `src/app/api/billing/webhook/route.ts:72-98,212-219`
- **Scenario**: `downgradeSubscription` guards against nuking "a hand-set `enterprise` via /api/org/plan" only when the subscription's product is NOT a known plan product. But the realistic collision is a KNOWN product: an org bought Pro via Polar, was later hand-upgraded to `enterprise` via the owner override (`ASCENT_ALLOW_PLAN_CHANGES`), and the now-redundant Pro subscription lapses/refunds — `setOrgPlan(org, "free")` unconditionally floors the org past its manual enterprise grant to `free`. The downgrade never compares the org's CURRENT plan to the tier the dying subscription conferred.
- **Root cause**: Downgrade target is a constant (`FREE_PLAN`) rather than "remove what THIS subscription granted"; the comment documents the unknown-product case as the hazard, leaving the known-product/manual-override overlap an unstated assumption ("orgs never mix override and subscription").
- **Impact**: An enterprise customer mid-contract (the exact population served by the manual override) loses unlimited scans, BYOM, and retention the moment a stale lower-tier subscription cancels — a silent, hard-to-diagnose entitlement outage with only a console.info trail.
- **Fix sketch**: In `downgradeSubscription` (and the full-refund branch), read the org's current plan first and only `setOrgPlan(org, "free")` when it equals `planForProduct(sub.productId)` (or is lower in `PLAN_ORDER`); log-and-skip when the current tier is higher than the subscription's, e.g. `current enterprise > sub pro — manual override retained`.

## 5. The pricing page contradicts its own metering engine on whether public scans consume the monthly allowance
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Category**: undocumented-assumption
- **File**: `src/lib/plans.ts:14-16,42-43`; `src/app/pricing/page.tsx:4-5,56-58`; vs `src/lib/db/credits.ts:3` and `src/components/pricing/creditMatrixData.ts:30`
- **Scenario**: The engine is unambiguous: credits.ts line 3 — "Public scans are free and unmetered; each PRIVATE… scan" is what draws allowance/credits, and the CreditMatrixLedger on the same /pricing page says public scans "never cost a credit on any plan". But `PLAN_FEATURES.free` markets "5 scans a month — public or private" (blurb + "Public or private repos" feature line), plans.ts documents `includedCredits` as "free scans/month (public OR private)", and the /pricing hero says "{5} free a month, then a subscription for more". The pricing page's own header comment still carries the OLD model ("private scans run on credits, 1 per scan; public scans are always free") — three generations of copy coexist on one public page.
- **Root cause**: The hybrid-allowance model replaced the credits-only model, and display copy in plans.ts/pricing was rewritten to "public or private" without reconciling against the metering code, which still exempts public scans entirely; no single statement records which behaviour is intended.
- **Impact**: The price cards UNDERSELL the free tier (it's actually 5 private scans + unlimited public) while the matrix below them says the opposite about public scans — a visitor comparing the two blocks on the same screen sees a direct contradiction, undermining exactly the "honest pricing" the page's comments aim for. It also leaves the next maintainer unsure whether metering public scans is a pending TODO or the copy is wrong.
- **Fix sketch**: Decide and record the model in one place (plans.ts doc comment): if public stays unmetered, change the Free blurb/features and hero to "5 private scans a month — public scans always free" (a stronger pitch anyway) and delete the stale old-model comment atop pricing/page.tsx; if public is meant to meter, fix credits.ts instead. Add a unit test asserting the blurb wording matches the metering flag to stop the next drift.

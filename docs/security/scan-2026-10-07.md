**Scope:** org authorization on the shared `public` org, read at `master` `b48499fa`. Two questions:

1. Does `requireOrgRole` let any signed-in account act as **owner** of the `public` org, which holds
   every public repo's scan series? It returned `null` (allowed) for `PUBLIC_ORG` at any `min`,
   right after `requireViewer` (`src/lib/authz.ts:298` at the base, `:308` now).
2. Do the `[id]` routes under `src/app/api/org/` gate on a caller-supplied org and then act on a row
   by id without proving the row belongs to that org (the class `656d96ff` fixed in the briefing
   share revoke)?

Not a question: `requireOrgAccess` admitting `PUBLIC_ORG` (`src/lib/authz.ts:174`) is the documented
free funnel (scan, import, watch). It was not changed. One of its callers is fixed (S7, tokens), because it
lets a stranger destroy shared public-org state.

Paths this pass was allowed to change: `src/lib/authz.ts`, `src/lib/authz.test.ts`, and
`src/app/api/org/{erase,retention,llm-provider,members,invites,tokens,credits}/`. Everything else is
reported with a proposed fix and was not touched.

## Verdict

**Question 1: confirmed, and fixed in every route within scope.** A signed-in account with no
membership anywhere passed `requireOrgRole("public", "owner")` and could, among other things, erase
the whole public corpus. Every claim below was proven by a route test that runs the **real**
`@/lib/authz` (only identity and data edges stubbed: login wall on, a signed-in viewer `stranger`,
no Membership row, every org already owned) and was **shown failing before its fix**, with
`expected 200 to be 403` and the mutation called.

The fix is one exported helper, `refusePublicOrgAdmin(org)` (`src/lib/authz.ts:350`), which answers
`403` for `public` (trimmed, case-insensitive) and `null` for every other org. Routes opt in.
`requireOrgRole`'s default is unchanged, so every caller outside these paths, including the
`hasOrgRole` reads on the report permalink and the executive tab, behaves exactly as before.
`authz.test.ts` pins that `requireOrgRole("public", "owner")` still returns `null`.

**Question 2: no finding.** All eight `[id]` routes bind the row to the gated org (table below).

Fourteen further owner/admin routes outside this pass's paths still admit `public` on a write. They
are listed under [Outside these paths](#outside-these-paths-reported-not-fixed) with proposed fixes.

## Residue the operator should check

The fixes stop new writes; they do not undo writes made before them. Rows a stranger may already have
left on the `public` org, each findable through its audit action:

- `Membership` rows on `public` (`org.member.role`, `org.member.invite_accepted`).
- Pending `Invite` rows on `public` (`org.member.invited`, `org.member.invite_resent`). An invite
  accepted through `/api/org/invites/accept` resolves its org from the token, so a pre-fix public
  invite can still be redeemed until it is revoked or expires.
- `OrgApiToken` rows on `public` (`org_api_token.created`).
- Retention overrides on the public `Organization` row (`retention.updated`).
- `OrgLlmConfig` on `public` (`org.llm_provider.updated`).
- Any `data.erased` entry for `public`: that would be S1 actually used.

## Method and limits

- Every route in the paths was read whole; each `route.public.test.ts` drives the real authz module
  rather than a mocked `requireOrgRole`, because a mocked gate would have proven nothing about the
  `PUBLIC_ORG` short-circuit, which is the defect.
- The out-of-path sweep is mechanical first (every `route.ts` calling `requireOrgRole`,
  `requireOrgOwnerPost`, or `requireOrgAccess` on a `DELETE`, with no `PUBLIC_ORG`/`"public"` test in
  the file), then read by hand at the gate. It does not trace each route's downstream consumer.
- Not covered: `src/app/api/org/{import,scan,watch,schedule,repos,active,repo-dimension}/` and
  `src/app/api/me/` (a full council reviews that surface next), `goals/` and `briefing/` (under
  council or waiting on the operator), pages and `src/features/**` beyond O14.
- `npm run build` was not run: Turbopack refuses the `node_modules` junction in a worktree.
